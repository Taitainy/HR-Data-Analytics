# HR-Data-Analytics
This project cotains interactive dashboards which provide a holistic HR analytics view that  shows the workforce structure, reveals employee sentiment and performance, and highlights development efforts.
## Key Insights
### Workforce Demography Overview
This analysis provides a clear snapshot of the organization’s employee composition. With a total of 2,845 employees, the average age is relatively high at 49.4 years, and the average tenure is 5.28 years, suggesting a mature and experienced workforce. Gender distribution is fairly balanced, with 56% male and 44% female employees. The age group analysis shows that adults form the largest segment, while younger employees are fewer, which may indicate challenges in attracting younger talent. Departmental distribution reveals that Production dominates with 1,910 employees, followed by IT/IS and Sales, while the Executive Office has the smallest headcount. Business units are evenly staffed, each hovering around 280–290 employees, reflecting a balanced organizational structure across divisions.
### Employee Performance & Engagement Dashboard
This shifts focus to employee sentiment and performance. Engagement, satisfaction, work-life balance, and employee ratings all hover around the mid-range (between 2.9 and 3.0), signaling moderate levels of employee morale. The engagement distribution shows that just over half of employees fall into the “moderate” category, with only about a quarter highly engaged, which could be a concern for long-term productivity. Departmental performance scores highlight Admin Offices as the top-performing group, while the Executive Office lags behind. Satisfaction scores vary widely across divisions, with Engineers and General Contractors showing strong numbers, while smaller divisions like Splicing and Wireline trail behind. Overall, the dashboard suggests that while performance is steady, there is room for improvement in boosting engagement and satisfaction across the board.
### Training & HR Investment
This dashboard captures the organization’s investment in employee development. With over $1.59 million spent on training across five programs, the completion rate is relatively low at 25.9%, raising questions about program effectiveness. Training outcomes are evenly split among completed, failed, incomplete, and passed categories, showing mixed success. Attendance data reveals a fairly balanced participation between internal and external programs. Departmental training costs are heavily skewed toward Production, which accounts for over $1 million, while IT/IS and Sales also receive significant investment. Training categories such as communication skills and project management show the highest costs, suggesting a focus on soft skills alongside technical development. Despite the large investment, the modest completion and success rates highlight a need to reassess training strategies to maximize impact.
### Overall Insight
Taken together, these analysis reveals a picture of a large, experienced workforce with balanced gender and departmental distribution. However, engagement and satisfaction levels are moderate, and training investments are not yielding strong completion rates. The organization may need to focus on attracting younger talent, improving employee morale, and optimizing training programs to ensure that investments translate into measurable performance gains.
## Recommendation
1. Partner with universities and professional bodies to attract younger talent, balancing the age distribution of the workforce.
2. Create mentorship programs where senior employees transfer knowledge to younger staff, ensuring continuity.
3. Redistribute training funds to ensure IT/IS and Sales receive adequate investment, supporting innovation and customer-facing excellence.
4. Launch recognition programs, flexible work policies, and wellness activities to quickly raise morale and engagement.
5. Align recruitment, engagement, and training into a unified talent strategy that supports organizational growth.
